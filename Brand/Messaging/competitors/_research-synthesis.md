## 1. Category positioning map

Two axes: emotional register (alarm ↔ calm) × agency (product acts ↔ user acts). Web data adds a fourth position.

**Lane A — Fear-first (product scares, product fixes):**
- Privacy Hawk: "Take Back Control of Your Privacy" + "Your private data is exposed in thousands of databases right now" (privacy-hawk-teardown.md). Web softens into authority: four printed US patent numbers, comparison table (privacyhawk.com, _landing-page-copy.md).
- Cloaked: "Threat level: High" (cloaked-teardown.md). **Web-vs-app delta:** web adds an empowerment layer the app lacks — "Cloaked empowers you to fight back" / "gives you tools to win" / "destroying the inventory that aggregators sell" (_landing-page-copy.md). Web hands you a weapon; the app hands down verdicts.
- Aura: app opens "$12.5 billion to fraud" (aura-teardown.md). **Web-vs-app delta:** homepage is calmer and feature-led — "Smart, simple online safety. Powered by AI." (_landing-page-copy.md).

**Lane B — Calm concierge (product reassures, product fixes):**
- PrivacyBee: "We find it. We remove it. We keep it gone." (privacybee-teardown.md). **Web-vs-app delta (reversed):** the web is *more* alarmist than the app — "Weaponizing Your Humanity Against You" / "Hackers Want Your Mother's Maiden Name" (_landing-page-copy.md). One brand, two registers.
- Guardio: "Don't worry, we'll take it from here" (guardio-teardown.md); web consistent — "Stay in control of your security" / "Browse, email, and text care-free" (guard.io, _landing-page-copy.md). Adds the AI-threat angle on web only.
- Incogni: "Take back control of your privacy" + "Total time saved 405 hrs" (incogni-teardown.md). Most app/web-consistent in the set; web sharpens proof with the Deloitte verification banner.
- DeleteMe (web-only): "We're ready to be your privacy partner." / "Privacy Advisors" — the only competitor that sells *people*, not software (_landing-page-copy.md).

**Lane C — User-as-actor (you drive):**
- Optery: "Consider if you'd like to submit the opt outs on your own self-service" (optery-teardown.md). **Web sharpens this** into a DIY-first funnel: "If you'd like to remove the profiles yourself, navigate to the self-service opt out pages. Don't have the time? Upgrade to a paid plan" (_landing-page-copy.md).
- Kasper: "Analyzing your visibility" (kasper-teardown.md). Web adds AI-source monitoring ("Bevakning av AI-källor" — ChatGPT, Gemini), a genuinely novel proof point (_landing-page-copy.md).
- Google: "Take control of results about you" with bounded promises (google-removal-request-teardown.md); support doc confirms restraint, adds the unique "Undo" affordance (_landing-page-copy.md).
- Mozilla Monitor (web-only): hero verb is "fix," not remove/protect/control — "Find where your private info is exposed — and take it back" / "data breach monitoring protection is always free" (monitor.mozilla.org, _landing-page-copy.md).

**Lane D — Investigation-as-consumer-product (new, Serus only):**
- Serus: "Privacy, agentic." / "See what the internet has on you—and take it down." / "Privacy is knowing what's out there. Intelligence is knowing who you're dealing with." / "One search before you pay, sign, meet, or trust." / "Some get to check. Everyone else guesses." (_landing-page-copy.md). The only competitor whose product is the *actor* ("agentic") while granting pipeline visibility: "You can watch every request move through the pipeline in the app." Closest functional competitor per James — and the closest tonal cousin to a Self-modeled Vanyshr.

Lanes A/B/C are product-as-actor or user-as-actor on *removal*. Serus is the only one selling *looking*.

## 2. Vocabulary findings

- **"Take control" is the category's master verb — confirmed on web too:** Aura product page, DeleteMe ("Take Control of Your Online Privacy"), Kasper ("Ta kontroll över ditt digitala fotavtryck"), Google (_landing-page-copy.md). Incogni's "take *back*" remains the sharpest variant, but contested (Hawk). Vanyshr needs a different verb or a sharper object.
- **Noun split unchanged, sharpened:** "exposure/exposed" dominates; Kasper's "visibility" ("övervaka din synlighet online") is still the only neutral, ownable noun (_landing-page-copy.md). Mozilla Monitor's hero verb "fix" is uncontested.
- **New ownable naming found on web:** Serus's "exposure list, ranked by risk", "Autopilot removal", "deployed agents", "monitored-source feed"; PrivacyBee's "Excruciating Details" (their most ownable naming — radical transparency as a brand word); Self's inability-framed "we literally cannot access your data."
- **Removal verbs:** remove / opt out / suppress (Incogni statuses); DeleteMe's "partner"/"Advisors" is the human-staffed outlier.
- **Ownable for Vanyshr:** "visibility" (uncontested, calm); locked term "synthetic" (category has no equivalent); Serus shows two-word product-line naming ("Privacy, agentic." / "Intelligence, agentic.") can carry a whole portfolio. Avoid "take control" and anything near it.

## 3. Tone clusters

- **Alarm-maximalist** (Hawk, Cloaked): Hawk's confirm-shaming ("No, stay exposed") has no web equivalent — the web softens to patents and comparison tables (privacy-hawk-teardown.md, _landing-page-copy.md). **Bee's web is the surprise:** the app's steady concierge becomes "Weaponizing Your Humanity Against You" online. Register splits are a deliberate web-vs-app choice, not an accident.
- **Warm guardian** (Guardio, Aura): Aura's web is calmer than its app ("Smart, simple online safety"); Guardio is the most consistent across surfaces.
- **Institutional-transparent** (Bee app, Optery, Google): Optery's honesty line is app-sourced (see v2 note); web's closest is "No Affiliation with Data Brokers" plus "Live Screenshots Prove Our Work" (_landing-page-copy.md). Google's support-doc honesty line — "When you remove a result... it doesn't mean that the info is gone from the internet" — is the category's most restrained sentence.
- **Quiet utility** (Kasper): unchanged; web adds AI-source monitoring without changing register.
- **Human-staffed** (DeleteMe, web-only): "privacy partner" / "Privacy Advisors" — warmth through people, not software.
- **Inability-framed** (Self, web-only): "We CANNOT see your AI conversations" / "We CANNOT decrypt your data (we don't have your keys)" — trust through *can't*, not *won't*. The strongest structural model for Vanyshr's voice.
- **Trust-builders:** bounded promises (Google's policy gates; Bee never claims "remove all"); honest failure states (Bee's rejected-request entry); DIY transparency (Bee's "DIY Removal Instructions", Optery's self-service pages, DeleteMe's opt-out-guides hub — **a web-native funnel, absent from every app**); justified data asks; Serus's pipeline visibility.
- **Trust-eroders:** confirm-shaming (Hawk); "remove all" maximalism; discount-led pricing on a trust product (Guardio "69% off"); labeling the user (Bee's "Vulnerable" badge); **unverified stat drift** (Aura's $12.5B → $21B).

## 4. Proof styles

- **Broker counts:** Incogni 420+ (now "Numbers verified by Deloitte"), Optery 950 sites, Hawk 265, Bee 1,135 (web title says 1,132 — consistent), Aura 425+ (_landing-page-copy.md).
- **Third-party verification (web-only escalation):** Incogni's Deloitte banner — "Independently verified by Deloitte... 245 million removal requests processed" — is the strongest proof in the category. Vanyshr has no equivalent yet; plan for one.
- **Patent race (web-only):** Hawk prints four US patent numbers (US-12164674-B1 et al.); Optery claims two. No app screenshot shows patents — patents are argued on the web, not in the product.
- **Verdict numbers:** Hawk "174 Exposures"; Bee "60/100 Medium Risk"; Guardio 93% gauge; Mozilla Monitor's "Data breaches happen every 11 minutes" — the most specific cadence stat in the set.
- **Work-done counters:** Incogni "Total time saved 405 hrs" + "245M+ data removal requests completed"; DeleteMe "141M+ Successful opt-out removals" + "3.6k+ Years of effort saved"; Aura "92 million removals, saving customers more than 23 million hours"; Guardio's live "blocked 3 dangerous websites since your last visit."
- **Named-competitor comparison tables (web-only):** Aura vs Incogni vs Cloaked grid; Hawk's "How We Compare." The web is where the category fights in public — no app equivalent exists.
- **Honesty-as-proof:** Incogni's sample-data tour; Optery's coverage caveats; Bee's "Excruciating Details" export-to-Excel; Google's "Undo."
- **Enterprise artifact (Intelbase lesson):** no proof points at all — just the live lookup demo. Working artifact > copy for enterprise buyers.

## 5. Funnel patterns

- **Dominant:** fear-stat → personal scan → paywall (Aura, Cloaked, Guardio, Hawk). Aura's product page sharpens the app's vague promises into process: "re-scans broker sites daily and re-submits opt-out requests if your data reappears" (_landing-page-copy.md).
- **Free-scan softening:** Incogni, Optery, Kasper ("Free forever"); Mozilla Monitor goes furthest — core offer permanently free.
- **DIY-first with convenience upsell:** Optery's web funnel ("remove the profiles yourself... Don't have the time? Upgrade") — its most distinctive conversion move, invisible in the app screenshots.
- **DIY-transparency hub:** DeleteMe's opt-out-guides hub + Bee's per-broker guides + Optery's self-service pages — a web-native trust funnel. Nobody in the set does this in-app.
- **Freemium ladder:** Hawk "10 Free Monthly AI Actions."
- **Waiting-room content:** Incogni's "Take a sneak peek inside Incogni while you wait" — the pattern for any async scan.
- **Household framing:** Guardio / Hawk family plans; Bee's Family tab; DeleteMe business solutions; Guardio Business tier.
- **Enterprise split (web-only pattern):** Serus (Protection/Intelligence), Incogni (Ironwall), DeleteMe (business), Guardio (Business), Self (enterprise section). Every scaled competitor segments consumer vs enterprise voice — directly relevant to the Intelbase "enterprise version of Vanyshr" framing.

## 6. The open lane for Vanyshr

- Nobody sounds like consumer fintech. The emotional range runs alarm → soothing → institutional → human-staffed. Nothing is light, quick, or balance-check casual.
- Nobody combines Optery's honesty + Aura's simplicity + user agency. Optery trusts the user's intelligence but drowns it in density; Aura explains simply but keeps the user helpless; Kasper is calm but never explains why.
- **Self reframes the model:** the product James wants to model isn't a removal tool at all — it's a private-AI super-app whose trust copy is inability-framed ("we literally cannot access your data") and whose relationship is user-as-key-holder. Vanyshr's agency lane should feel like *that*, not like a better dashboard.
- **Serus is the one to beat on positioning craft:** "Privacy, agentic." / "Intelligence, agentic." is the tightest product-line naming in the set, and "See what the internet has on you—and take it down" hands the user the verb. Vanyshr needs a one-liner at that altitude.
- **The lane:** quantified proof + calm confidence + user agency — privacy as easy as checking a balance, with inability-framed trust ("can't" not "won't") and pipeline visibility (Serus's "watch every request move through the pipeline").
- **Locked-direction fit:** Cash App-inspired approachable fintech; system fonts in-product; "synthetic" as the owned term; Self as product model (Lane C agency + Self's key-holder relationship); Intelbase as enterprise analogue (artifact > copy); Serus as closest functional competitor. The web research sharpens the contrast: Vanyshr takes Lane C's agency, Lane B's calm, Self's inability-framing — and leaves Lane A's fear to Hawk and Cloaked.

## 6b. James's differentiation thesis (2026-09-24 — feed the workshop + copy)

Captured from James directly; these are the points the copy must make legible to end users:

- **Architecture split — retrieval vs. execution (James's theory, anecdotal, UNPROVEN).** Sophisticated web scrapers pull the data programmatically (no agents for retrieval). Agents do the *doing*: removing data, managing communications with brokers, orchestrating the removal pipeline. The user never interacts with the agents. James's *unproven* read of Serus: their "agent" retrieves data to feed a chatbot — the loop ends at answers, not removals. Do not let copy lean on this until verified.
- **Solo founder — explicitly NOT consumer copy.** James: users don't care if 1 or a million people built it; one-person product would hurt consumer marketing. Its value is (a) margin/viability advantage and (b) the VC/funding/buyout narrative. Possible founder-led social toward indie-tech audiences (not ICP) — never in consumer marketing.
- **Family-first hypothesis — UNVALIDATED, needs real data, James not married to it.** Lead with a generous family plan (~10 users); competitors treat family as a throwaway add-on to individual plans. Must be tested on two axes: (a) does it sell at better CAC, (b) scalability/unit economics — removal work is expensive per user, 10 users multiplies cost per subscription.
- **THE differentiator: synthetic-data obfuscation ("poisoning the well").** No competitor in the set offers it. Locked term: "synthetic." Register split: "poisoning the well" for deeper copy/socials, "Synthetic"-led on CTA pages.
- **Messaging stance:** never "their product is shit" — always "my product is noticeably better." Differentiation by proof, not by tearing down competitors.

## 7. Frameworks structuring the workshop

From `_framework-research.md` — one line each, no rehash:
- **NN/g Four Dimensions of Tone** (Funny↔Serious, Formal↔Casual, Respectful↔Irreverent, Enthusiastic↔Matter-of-fact): positions as ratios (80/20), never unexplained midpoints; Monzo's Casual/Serious/Respectful/Enthusiastic as the starting hypothesis.
- **Voice-vs-tone + "this, but not that"** (Mailchimp/Slack): voice is constant, tone moves with stakes; every trait gets its "not that" bound — this is the we-are/we-are-not shape.
- **Messaging house** (tagline → value prop → exactly 3 pillars → checkable proof): Moore positioning template tested against five filters (specific, differentiated, true, relevant, durable); skeptic test + ladder-up test for pillars.
- **JTBD (functional / emotional / social):** everyone sells the functional job ("remove my data"); the emotional job ("stop worrying, feel in control") is Vanyshr's lane; the social job ("be someone who takes privacy seriously") is untapped.
- **Monzo + Cash App:** Monzo's four principles (straightforward kindness, everyday magic, warm wit, reader-first — "what does my reader care about most?") are the worked example; Cash App validates the locked philosophy — trust through insider language and restraint, not explanation.

## 8. Workshop-ready comparison questions

1. (Positioning) Hawk: "Take Back Control of Your Privacy" through fear; Serus: "See what the internet has on you—and take it down." through agency. Does Vanyshr's one-liner alarm or hand over the verb — and can it hit Serus's altitude?
2. (Positioning) Serus sells *looking* ("One search before you pay, sign, meet, or trust"); everyone else sells *removing*. Is Vanyshr a removal tool with visibility, or a visibility tool with removal?
3. (Vocabulary) The category says "exposure"; Kasper says "visibility"; Mozilla Monitor says "fix." Victim noun, neutral noun, or repair verb?
4. (Vocabulary) "Take control" is used by Aura, Google, Guardio, DeleteMe, and Kasper (in Swedish). What verb owns Vanyshr's core action — and can "synthetic" carry feature-level naming on its own?
5. (Tone) Optery argues against its own revenue instincts (app-sourced honesty line); Hawk claims it can "remove all of your sensitive personal information." How bounded are Vanyshr's promises — and do we go as far as Self's "we literally cannot"?
6. (Tone/trust) Bee's app shows failure states honestly ("Company has rejected your exposure request"); its web runs "Weaponizing Your Humanity Against You." One brand, two registers — where does Vanyshr's web voice sit vs its in-app voice?
7. (Proof) Incogni proves with Deloitte verification; Hawk with four printed patents; Optery with live screenshots. Authority, paperwork, or radical transparency — what proves Vanyshr?
8. (Funnel) Optery, Bee, and DeleteMe all publish DIY transparency (self-service pages, "Excruciating Details," opt-out guides) — teaching people to leave. Does Vanyshr teach the DIY path and trust users to stay?
9. (Agency) Guardio: "Don't worry, we'll take it from here" (passenger); Optery: "submit the opt outs on your own self-service" (driver); Self: "Your Data, Your Keys, Your Control" (key-holder). Passenger, driver, or key-holder?
10. (Enterprise) Every scaled competitor splits consumer/enterprise voice (Ironwall, Serus Intelligence, Guardio Business). Intelbase sells enterprise with a demo, not copy. One voice with two registers, or two voices — and what does Vanyshr's enterprise artifact look like?
11. (Education) Aura teaches first ("Ever heard of data brokers?"); Kasper never explains why. How much education before Vanyshr's scan — and is the teacher more Monzo ("straightforward kindness") or Cash App (no explainer at all)?
