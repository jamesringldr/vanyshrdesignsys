# Adjacent intelligence: Carefull + InfoArmor/Allstate Identity Protection

> **What this is:** profiles of two advisor-distributed identity/fraud-protection services James hadn't heard of, surfaced by the partnership recon. · **Created:** 2026-09-24 · **Status:** research complete.

## Framing note — why this is filed as adjacent intelligence, not a core competitor

Neither Carefull nor InfoArmor is a data-broker-removal service. **Carefull** is an elder-focused financial-safety/fraud-monitoring platform distributed through advisors and financial institutions. **InfoArmor** was the employee-benefits identity-protection company Allstate acquired in 2018 and rebranded as Allstate Identity Protection. What matters for Vanyshr is not their product (Vanyshr doesn't compete with either head-on) but three things: (1) they **prove the advisor-distribution channel buys this category of product right now**, with public deal terms; (2) they define the generic "protection" promise Vanyshr's copy must differentiate against; and (3) they reveal the whitespace — **nobody in this channel does removal execution or anything like synthetic obfuscation**.

## Carefull

**What it is:** "the first financial safety platform designed to protect aging adults, their families, and financial advisors from elder fraud, scams, and money mistakes" (company boilerplate, [businesswire.com](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250507010840/en/Integrated-Partners-Launches-Strategic-Partnership-With-Carefull-to-Protect-Older-Clients-From-Fraud)). Founded 2019 by Max Goldman and Todd Rovak; raised ~$20M total ([advisorhub.com](https://www.advisorhub.com/edward-jones-invests-in-elder-fraud-prevention-startup/)).

**Who it's for:** older adults plus the 45M U.S. "financial caregivers" — adults managing an older loved one's daily finances — plus the financial institutions and advisors that serve them ([getcarefull.com](https://getcarefull.com)).

**Product:** AI account oversight (24/7 monitoring of transactions, credit activity, behavioral patterns, home titles), ScamCheck (scam-message verification tool), identity and credit protection, trusted-contact coordination (view-only alert sharing), secure document/password vault (LegacyKit/Vault), a U.S.-based Care Team that intervenes during scam attempts, and $1M identity theft insurance. **Note: the advisor product page lists "exposed personal information" among things it monitors — it flags exposure; it does not remove anything** ([getcarefull.com/cetera](https://getcarefull.com/cetera)).

**Distribution:** B2B2C — sold to firms, offered to clients as a benefit. Verified deals:

- **Edward Jones × Carefull (June 24, 2026)** — commercial relationship; free to all ~9M U.S. clients via ~20,000 advisors, plus a minority investment through Edward Jones Ventures ([advisorhub.com](https://www.advisorhub.com/edward-jones-invests-in-elder-fraud-prevention-startup/); PR: [prnewswire via millismedwaynews](https://pr.millismedwaynews.com/article/Edward-Jones-Introduces-Carefull-Financial-Safety-Platform-to-Help-Families-Protect-What-Matters-Most/6a3bb989564d536c69a53896)). - **Integrated Partners × Carefull (May 7, 2025)** — $21B AUA RIA; includes an advisor real-time engagement portal ([businesswire.com](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250507010840/en/Integrated-Partners-Launches-Strategic-Partnership-With-Carefull-to-Protect-Older-Clients-From-Fraud)). Andree Mohr (Integrated): *"Choosing Carefull as a strategic partner allows our advisors to offer a high-touch service that protects clients from real-world threats of fraud, scams and money mistakes."* Rovak: *"protecting wealth means protecting more than assets — it means safeguarding the trust, identity and daily financial life of every client."*

**Pricing:** no current public direct-to-consumer price (the site is now B2B). A 2021 partner page quoted $29.99/mo list with free for bank customers ([partner.getcarefull.com/tcb/home](https://partner.getcarefull.com/tcb/home)). Edward Jones is firm-paid (free to clients); other firm deals are negotiated. Current wholesale rates: **not publicly listed — unverifiable.**

## InfoArmor → Allstate Identity Protection

**Verification of what this brand is today:** James's read is correct. Allstate announced the acquisition August 27, 2018 and **closed it October 5, 2018 for $525M** ([global-benefits-vision.com](https://www.global-benefits-vision.com/allstate-acquires-identity-theft-services-company-infoarmor/); [allstateidentityprotection.com](https://www.allstateidentityprotection.com/business/content-hub/allstate-closes-acquisition-of-infoarmor)). InfoArmor (Scottsdale, AZ; products PrivacyArmor identity monitoring + VigilanteATI threat intel) served 1M+ employees at 1,400+ firms including 100+ Fortune 500 at acquisition. The InfoArmor brand was retired for customers: *"InfoArmor is now Allstate Identity Protection"* ([ease.com](https://www.ease.com/marketplace/allstate-identity-protection/)); job listings read *"We are Allstate Identity Protection (formerly, InfoArmor), a wholly owned subsidiary of Allstate since 2019."* The legal entity (InfoArmor, Inc.) still operates the service behind the Allstate brand.

**Product (current, Allstate Identity Protection):** identity/credit monitoring, dark web monitoring, fraud alerts, full-service restoration, $1M insurance, distributed mainly through employee benefits (3,000+ companies, 30%+ of the Fortune 500). Its privacy-management tool is **Allstate Digital Footprint**, described verbatim as: *"a first-of-it's kind privacy management tool. Now members can see their personal information and how it might expose them to risk — so they can do things no other identity protection provider has helped them do before"* ([ease.com](https://www.ease.com/marketplace/allstate-identity-protection/)). Note: visibility only — no removal execution.

**Baird × InfoArmor — verified, with an important correction:** the partnership recon's $7.95/mo figure checks out against Baird's 2018 client flyer (Broadridge): *"Baird has partnered with InfoArmor, an expert in identity theft protection, to provide our clients with identity protection and fraud detection services. This service is available for as little as $7.95 per month for Baird clients – a significant discount from standard rates."* Features: high-risk transaction protection, credit monitoring/scores, dark web surveillance, digital wallet storage, SocialArmor social media monitoring, full-service restoration, $1M identity theft insurance. Enrollment runs through the advisor: *"Please contact your Baird Financial Advisor for enrollment details"* ([broadridgeadvisor.com PDF](https://mediahandler.broadridgeadvisor.com/media/238761/150900%20PROTECTING%20YOURSELF%20CLIENT.pdf?MediaVersion=1&optimize=false)). Historical InfoArmor list pricing: $7.95/mo individual / $13.95 family ([sbam.org brochure](https://www.sbam.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/InfoArmour-Brochure-091619.pdf)) vs. $14.95/$24.95 direct retail — the Baird "discount" was the benefits-channel base rate.

**The correction:** Baird's *current* offering (bairdwealth.com, ©2024) is **ID Watchdog (Equifax)**, not InfoArmor — *"Take action with award-winning ID Watchdog identity theft protection,"* Special Pricing Single $9.95 / Family $17.95 ([bairdwealth.com PDF](https://www.bairdwealth.com/globalassets/pdfs/help/identity-theft-protection-infoarmor-overview.pdf)). The Baird × InfoArmor deal is 2018-era; the door moved to an Equifax product. Two implications: (1) these advisor doors **re-bid** — a better product can displace the incumbent; (2) ID Watchdog's bundle now includes *"Digital Privacy Scans & Removal"* — hedged by fine print: *"There is no guarantee that we can detect or remove consumer personal information from all people search sites."* That hedge is the category's honest ceiling on removal, and it names Vanyshr's opening.

## Vanyshr analysis (Igor's synthesis — analysis, not sourced fact)

**(a) Where they overlap Vanyshr.** The elder-scam/fraud-victim ICP: Carefull owns elder-fraud positioning *through the advisor channel* — the same channel Vanyshr would sell through, to overlapping clients. The generic "protection" promise overlaps too: monitoring, alerts, insurance, "peace of mind" (Carefull uses the phrase; Vanyshr's VoC bank flags it as generic-headline language to avoid). If an advisor already gives clients Carefull, Vanyshr's pitch must answer "don't I already have this?" — and the answer is that Carefull watches the accounts while Vanyshr shrinks the attack surface itself.

**(b) Where they don't.** Both are monitor-and-alert, not removal execution. Carefull flags "exposed personal information" and does nothing about it. Allstate's Digital Footprint is see-your-data visibility. Neither does anything like synthetic obfuscation — their answer to exposure is watching; Vanyshr's is *poisoning the well*. The ID Watchdog fine print ("no guarantee… remove… from all people search sites") is the industry's own admission that removal is the unsolved layer. Vanyshr's synthetic angle doesn't promise perfect deletion either — it makes circulating copies *unreliable*, which is a structurally different (and more honest) claim than anything in this channel.

**(c) Channel intelligence — what their advisor pitch teaches Vanyshr.** Carefull's advisor page is the template: **pitch the firm's economics, never the client's fear.** Retention, next-gen leads ("9 in 10 people will not use their parents' advisor"), "Positive ROI from Month 1," and crucially — *"advisors are not in the alert flow"* / *"we do the work… your brand gets all the credit."* Zero advisor labor is the deal-maker. Vanyshr's advisor pitch should mirror that structure: client-perk economics + zero advisor labor + the referral-upside story (FA-Mag's ~1 referral/month/$1M benchmark from the partnership recon). The positioning gap: **every advisor-distributed product is monitor-and-alert — the privacy/removal layer is nobody's.** Vanyshr pitches as the layer Carefull and ID Watchdog don't have. Also note Carefull's proof asset: real-time advisor portal tracking client engagement — Vanyshr will need an equivalent (advisor-visible proof, not just client-visible).

**(d) Partner vs. rival.** **Carefull: plausible future partner, not a rival.** They monitor accounts; Vanyshr removes broker data and poisons copies; both serve the elder/family ICP through the same advisor channel. A "financial safety + data privacy" bundle is coherent, and Carefull has no removal DNA to build it themselves. Worth a conversation once Vanyshr has one referenceable advisor deployment. **InfoArmor/Allstate: not a partner for a solo founder** — a Fortune-500 benefits machine; the lesson is the channel, not the deal. **ID Watchdog/Equifax: channel competitor to displace eventually, not partner** — but the Baird migration proves these doors re-bid, and a product that actually does the removal layer ID Watchdog hedges on is the displacement wedge.
