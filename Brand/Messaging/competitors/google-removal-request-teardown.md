# Competitor Teardown: Google — Results About You / Detailed Removal Request

> **What this is:** messaging teardown of Google's "Results about you" removal-request flow for Vanyshr brand-voice research · **Analyzed:** 2026-09-24 · **Sources:** 14 screenshots (`Google Detailed Removal Request/Google Detailed Removal Request/`; several are Chrome UI/account screens with no product copy and are excluded), support URL https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/12719076?hl=en and product URL https://myactivity.google.com/results-about-you/home (page text not pulled — browser fetch failed; screenshots only). Screenshots show the reviewer's Google accounts — described generically, never quoted.

## Identity

- **Product:** Google "Results about you" — first-party flow to find personal info in Google Search results, get notified of new appearances, and request removals
- **URL:** https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/12719076?hl=en
- **Category / who it's for:** any Google account holder with personal info (name, contact info, ID numbers) appearing in Search

## Positioning

- Their one-liner (quoted): "Take control of results about you" (`Screenshot 2026-09-18 at 9.33.15 PM.png`)
- Who they claim to serve: the individual search subject — "We're here to help you find personal info that shows up on Google Search. You can ask to remove any search results you'd rather keep private."

## Voice observations

- Formality (1–5): 4 — institutional, plain, careful. No contractions in key promises, no marketing adjectives.
- Playfulness (1–5): 1 — none.
- Confidence (1–5): 3 — deliberately *bounded* confidence. This is the only competitor that openly limits its own promise: "If you make a removal request that meets policy requirements, Google will remove the result for everyone." (`Screenshot 2026-09-18 at 9.33.39 PM.png`)
- Notes: Google's voice is **trust-through-restraint**. Every data ask carries its justification inline: "Google only uses this to help you find and remove personal info, and to test and improve the system for doing that. Your info will be protected through advanced encryption." (`Screenshot 2026-09-18 at 9.33.55 PM.png`). The FAQ answers "What does Google do with the personal info you provide for monitoring?" with "we understand that you trust us with your sensitive information. We take that responsibility very seriously… We don't share your info or use it to personalize your experience across other Google products." (`Screenshot 2026-09-18 at 9.36.12 PM.png`). No fear, no stats, no upsell — it can't afford to sound like a vendor.

## Messaging pillars (what they repeat)

1. **Control over *your* results.** "Take control of results about you" — the scope is explicitly Search results, nothing more.
2. **Monitoring as a quiet background job.** "Add info, get notified — We can run regular checks for the info you care about, and let you know if it shows up in search results." (`Screenshot 2026-09-18 at 9.33.24 PM.png`)
3. **Justified data asks.** Each PII request (name, contact info, ID numbers) is paired with a use-limitation sentence and an encryption reassurance at the point of entry.
4. **Policy honesty.** Removal is conditional ("meets policy requirements"); monitoring use is enumerated ("Monitoring · To process removal requests · To improve the monitoring and removal process · To let you find the status of your removal requests over time").

## Visual-verbal link

- The design is standard Google settings UI — deliberately invisible. The copy *is* the product surface, and its plainness matches: no brand theater, just labeled steps. Match by design; the restraint is the point.

## For Vanyshr

- **Steal:** the inline data-use justification at the moment of asking for PII — "Google only uses this to help you find and remove personal info" is the pattern Vanyshr should copy verbatim in spirit everywhere it asks for name, email, or phone. Nobody else in the set justifies asks this cleanly. Also the bounded promise ("meets policy requirements") — saying what you *can't* do builds more trust than the category's usual infinite guarantees.
- **Avoid:** institutional coldness — Google can afford to sound like a utility; Vanyshr can't. And don't imply parity with "remove the result for everyone": that's a Google-only superpower over its own index.
- **Differentiation note:** Google owns exactly one layer — its own search results — and its voice concedes everything else by omission. Every broker, dark-web, and people-search claim in the category sits outside Google's frame. Vanyshr's whole pitch ("everything Google doesn't cover, in one tool") fits in the space this voice leaves blank. Note the framing overlap with James's "closest functional competitor" Serus and the Self product model: none of the screenshot competitors explain *search-result* removal this plainly — a Vanyshr explainer could borrow Google's restraint without its coldness.
