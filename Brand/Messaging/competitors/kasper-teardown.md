# Competitor Teardown: Kasper

> **What this is:** messaging teardown of Kasper for Vanyshr brand-voice research · **Analyzed:** 2026-09-24 · **Sources:** 6 screenshots (`Kasper/Kasper /`, web app at app.kasper.app), URL kasper.app (page text not pulled — browser fetch failed; screenshots only). Screenshots show the reviewer's name in form fields — described generically, never quoted.

## Identity

- **Product:** Kasper — personal-data visibility and removal across "search sites and search engines"; Personal/Business tiers; freemium
- **URL:** kasper.app
- **Category / who it's for:** individuals and businesses who want to know — and reduce — what the internet shows about them

## Positioning

- Their one-liner (quoted): no marketing one-liner appears in the screenshots; the functional equivalent is "Analyzing your visibility — We are analyzing your visibility on search sites and search engines. You will be notified when the analysis is complete." (`Screenshot 2026-09-09 at 9.00.23 PM.png`)
- Who they claim to serve: anyone with a searchable footprint — the "Personal | Business" toggle on the login screen (`Screenshot 2026-09-09 at 8.58.02 PM.png`) is the only audience statement.

## Voice observations

- Formality (1–5): 4 — the most formal in the set; complete sentences, no contractions in product copy, no colloquialisms.
- Playfulness (1–5): 1 — none whatsoever.
- Confidence (1–5): 3 — quiet rather than assertive. It states function without selling it.
- Notes: Kasper is the **quiet utility** — nearly zero persuasion copy anywhere in the funnel. The setup wizard ("Finish setup — Verify | Name | Country | Info | Plan") assumes intent; the product tabs ("Home | Sites | Removal | AI Visibility | Monitor") are the pitch. Its distinctive noun is **"visibility"** — a neutral, almost positive framing where everyone else says "exposure." "AI Visibility" as a tab is the most forward-looking feature label in the set.

## Messaging pillars (what they repeat)

1. **Visibility, not exposure.** The core noun throughout: "Analyzing your visibility," "Overview of your visibility." It frames the problem as something you *manage*, not something done to you.
2. **Search as the battleground.** "search sites and search engines" — narrower and more concrete than "the dark web."
3. **Freemium clarity.** "Free — $0 — Free forever" / "Included in Free: Overview of your visibility" / "Select your plan — Choose the plan that suits you best" (`Open a New Browser Tab.png`).
4. **Gated depth.** "Data Leaks — Upgrade to unlock — This feature requires the Plus plan or higher. — Upgrade to Plus" (`Screenshot 2026-09-09 at 9.02.10 PM.png`).

## Visual-verbal link

- Copy matches the design vibe: stark black/white minimalism, no illustration, no color storytelling. The words are as spare as the UI — functional labels, short declaratives. No clash; if anything the brand under-writes, leaving the visuals to carry an Apple-like severity the copy doesn't earn or need.

## For Vanyshr

- **Steal:** "visibility" as the category noun — it's ownable, neutral-to-positive, and sidesteps the victim framing of "exposure." Worth testing in the workshop against "exposure." Also the free-forever "overview" hook: give the scan away, charge for the fix.
- **Avoid:** the persuasion vacuum — Kasper never explains *why* visibility matters or what a data broker is; it converts only the already-convinced. Vanyshr needs Aura's education beat with Kasper's calm.
- **Differentiation note:** Kasper is the quiet utility for the self-directed. Its "AI Visibility" tab points at the same AI-search-exposure territory as Intelbase ("the enterprise version of vanyshr" per James) — flag for the research pass: whoever owns plain-language AI-visibility messaging for consumers first has an open lane, since Kasper labels it but doesn't explain it.
