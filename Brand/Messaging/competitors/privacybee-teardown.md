# Competitor Teardown: PrivacyBee

> **What this is:** messaging teardown of PrivacyBee for Vanyshr brand-voice research · **Analyzed:** 2026-09-24 · **Sources:** privacybee-appshots-01.png through privacybee-appshots-05.png (5 collage images, 26 app screens total, in `~/workspace/your_files/vanyshr-brand/uploads/PrivacyBee/`); https://privacybee.com/ (landing page URL on file, not yet fetched — app screenshots are the primary evidence)

## Identity

- **Product:** PrivacyBee — consumer privacy app: data-broker scanning/removal, digital-footprint monitoring, identity vault, dark-web monitoring, family protection
- **URL:** https://privacybee.com/
- **Category / who it's for:** Consumer data-removal / privacy protection for individuals and families — family is a first-class audience with its own onboarding tour screen, bottom-nav "Family" tab, and per-member license model

## Positioning

- Their one-liner (quoted): "We find it. We remove it. We keep it gone." (privacybee-appshots-01.png, onboarding explainer screen, followed by "Here's how Privacy Bee protects you:")
- Who they claim to serve: everyday consumers who want an expert team to handle removals — "Our team sends removal requests on your behalf and follows up until your data is deleted" (privacybee-appshots-01.png) — plus families ("Protect the people you care about most. Add family members to your plan and we'll scan, remove, and monitor their exposed data too—all managed from your account", privacybee-appshots-03.png) and high-risk individuals (the $799.99/yr "Signature" tier is explicitly "for individuals in crisis or at high risk", privacybee-appshots-05.png)

## Voice observations

- Formality (1–5): 3 — plain but structured and explanatory; more professional and polished than Privacy Hawk's street-level 2. Instructional clarity over punchiness.
- Playfulness (1–5): 2 — mostly serious; the bee mascot and "Let's Explore!" tour headers are the only lightness. No jokes in the product voice.
- Confidence (1–5): 3 — assertive but bounded. Makes process promises ("follows up until your data is deleted") rather than absolute ones, and — notably — shows failure states honestly: "Company has rejected your exposure request. Reason: 'Missing signed authorization authorization (LPOA)'." (privacybee-appshots-03.png)
- Notes: Calm, institutional, process-oriented. The signature move is teaching the user what's happening: the recurring "What happens next" 3-step status card ("1 Detected — Found your data on this site / 2 In progress — Removal request sent / 3 Removed — We'll confirm once removed", privacybee-appshots-03.png and 04.png) and per-broker "Opt-Out Guide" articles with "DIY Removal Instructions". Risks are stated once in onboarding ("THIS PUTS YOU AT RISK FOR: Identity theft & fraud · Endless spam calls & texts · Unwanted mail & email · Doxxing & cyberstalking", privacybee-appshots-01.png), then the app voice stays steady — "We handled this for you", "No action required". Compared to Privacy Hawk's fear-first scare→quantify→sell machinery, Bee is the composed concierge: it never shames, never guilt-trips at the paywall, and never claims it can "remove all" of anything.

## Messaging pillars (what they repeat)

1. **The 3-step loop: find → remove → keep gone.** The mantra "We find it. We remove it. We keep it gone." (privacybee-appshots-01.png) is operationalized everywhere: onboarding's "1 Scan — We search thousands of data brokers, people-search sites, and companies for your personal information. 2 Remove — Our team sends removal requests on your behalf and follows up until your data is deleted. 3 Monitor — 24/7 continuous scanning ensures your data stays private. New threats are handled as they appear." (privacybee-appshots-01.png), and the "What happens next" status card on every exposure detail.
2. **Quantified risk as a recurring habit.** "Your September Privacy Risk Score — A quick look at your current privacy health" with "60 /100" and "Your Privacy Risk Score is Medium Risk" plus "How is my Privacy Risk Score determined?" (privacybee-appshots-03.png); scan-results verdict "Your Privacy Risk is HIGH" with "90 pieces of your info across 7 sites" (privacybee-appshots-02.png); dashboard stats "Scans Performed 85", "Exposures Found (includes likely) 382" (privacybee-appshots-03.png). The monthly-cadence score framing turns anxiety into a check-in ritual.
3. **The Identity Vault — a proactive data model.** "Identity Vault — Securely store and manage your personal identifiers in one place. Add records like your name, email addresses, phone numbers, and more—everything that represents you online." (privacybee-appshots-02.png). Distinct from competitors: they ask you to declare your identity surface up front so matching is precise, rather than scanning first and asking later.
4. **Family protection as a core tab, not an upsell.** "Family Protection — Protect the people you care about most... all managed from your account" (privacybee-appshots-03.png); "Family" sits in the bottom nav next to Dashboard, Identity Vault, Data Brokers, Presence; per-member rows show "Exposures 49 / In Progress 11 / Removals 13" and license states ("Complete License", "Essentials License").
5. **Radical process transparency.** Honest pipeline states everywhere: "Paused" badges on removals, "May remain in progress until data brokers process the request and confirm removal" (privacybee-appshots-03.png), the rejected-request activity entry with the broker's stated reason, and per-site "Opt-Out Guide" articles ("ReversePhone Opt-Out Guide: How to Remove Your Personal Information" with "Overview", "What is ReversePhone?", "Types of personal information they expose") plus a "DIY Removal Instructions →" link even on paid screens (privacybee-appshots-04.png). This is the opposite of Hawk's black-box automation.

## Visual-verbal link

- The copy and design are well matched: calm navy-and-white UI, friendly bee mascot, clean stat cards, and a voice that is steady, explanatory, and institutional — "We handled this for you" reads like a bank telling you the transfer cleared. The "⚠ Vulnerable" badge pinned in the app header keeps a low-grade alertness without Hawk's red-siren theater.
- Where it clashes: the onboarding fear list ("Doxxing & cyberstalking") is Hawk-adjacent alarm copy that the rest of the app never matches again — the product voice immediately downshifts to concierge. And two typos in trust-critical copy dent the polished-institutional posture: "sharing publically your personal information" (privacybee-appshots-01.png) and "Missing signed authorization authorization (LPOA)" (privacybee-appshots-03.png).

## For Vanyshr

- **Steal:** The "What happens next" 3-step status card (Detected → In progress → Removed) — it teaches the process, sets expectations on timelines ("May remain in progress until data brokers process the request"), and turns waiting into confidence. The monthly Privacy Risk Score cadence ("Your September Privacy Risk Score") is a strong retention-and-ritual mechanic. Per-broker opt-out guides with honest "DIY Removal Instructions" even for non-payers is the most trust-building move in the whole competitor set — it says "we win on convenience, not on hiding the manual path." And "We find it. We remove it. We keep it gone." is the cleanest structural model for a three-beat promise Vanyshr could write its own version of.
- **Avoid:** "Vulnerable" as a persistent header badge — it labels the user with a shame-adjacent word on every screen; Vanyshr's approachable-fintech direction should never brand the user as the problem. The tier jump from Pro ($199.99/yr) to Signature ($799.99/yr) with "crisis or at high risk" framing — crisis-priced tiers are a tone Vanyshr should not borrow. Proofread everything twice; typos in trust copy ("publically", "authorization authorization") are credibility leaks.
- **Differentiation note:** what space does this leave open for us? PrivacyBee owns the calm-institutional concierge lane ("our team handles it, here's the process") and Privacy Hawk owns scared-straight ("you are exposed, act now"). Both converge on the same quantified-verdict reporting — risk scores, exposure counts, gauge dials. Neither gives the user real agency: both are "we handle it for you" products with the user watching a dashboard. Combined with Kasper's "visibility" vocabulary and Optery's DIY path from the sibling teardowns, the genuinely open lane for Vanyshr is **quantified proof + calm confidence + user agency** — the user as the actor with a clear, simple control surface, not a passive dashboard watcher and not a frightened convert. That maps directly onto the locked direction: Cash App-like — robust capability that feels almost rudimentary to use.
