# Competitor Teardown: Cloaked

> **What this is:** messaging teardown of Cloaked for Vanyshr brand-voice research · **Analyzed:** 2026-09-24 · **Sources:** 4 app screenshots (`Cloaked/Cloaked/`), URL https://www.cloaked.com/ (page text not pulled — browser fetch failed; screenshots only). Note: screenshots show the reviewer's real personal data (name, DOB, phone, addresses, relatives) — PII is described generically here, never quoted.

## Identity

- **Product:** Cloaked — data-broker removal + personal-data protection app; scan shows exposure, paywall-gated removal
- **URL:** https://www.cloaked.com/
- **Category / who it's for:** privacy-concerned consumers; the scan flow personalizes hard around the individual's exposed records

## Positioning

- Their one-liner (quoted): no single hero one-liner in the screenshots; the closest is the scan verdict "Threat level: High" (`Screenshot 2026-09-09 at 9.20.30 PM.png`) and the CTA "Remove and Protect My Data."
- Who they claim to serve: individuals whose personal records (SSN, DOB, phone, addresses, associates) are circulating among data brokers — the product speaks to *your* file, not a generic threat.

## Voice observations

- Formality (1–5): 3 — direct declarative sentences, no slang, no stiffness. "U.S. and Canadian phone numbers accepted."
- Playfulness (1–5): 1 — none. Dark UI, serif display type, funereal tone.
- Confidence (1–5): 5 — the most certain voice in the set. It does not hedge: "A lot of your vulnerable information was accessible and found on 100+ data brokers." Labels are verdicts: "High Personal Risk," "Safety Risk," "Spam Risk."
- Notes: Cloaked's signature move is **threat-model vocabulary applied to a consumer**. "Threat level," "Personal Risk," "Safety Risk," "Spam Risk" — terms borrowed from security operations, rendered as pill badges. The copy explains *consequences*, not mechanisms: "Fraudsters with this information can apply for: Global Entry Status; a REAL ID License; an IRS Tax Refund; even a new bank account or Credit Card." (`Screenshot 2026-09-09 at 9.20.30 PM.png`). Second-person consequences ("subjecting you to unwanted advertising and dangerous phishing attempts") keep it personal.

## Messaging pillars (what they repeat)

1. **Your exposure, quantified and labeled.** "Threat level: High," "found on 100+ data brokers," risk pills per category. The scan *is* the pitch.
2. **Consequence-first explanations.** Every data type gets a "what criminals can do with this" caption — fraudsters, scammers, spammers each get their own paragraph.
3. **Remove + protect as one action.** The single CTA "Remove and Protect My Data" fuses deletion and ongoing defense; no separate "monitor" upsell in the funnel shown.

## Visual-verbal link

- Copy matches the design vibe completely: near-black UI, serif headlines ("Scan details," "Create account," "Choose your plan"), red/amber risk pills. It reads like a security briefing — the words and the visuals agree that this is serious. No clash; if anything the design is *more* severe than the copy, which stays plain-spoken.

## For Vanyshr

- **Steal:** consequence-first microcopy. "Fraudsters with this information can apply for…" is the best example in the set of making abstract exposure concrete — Vanyshr's scan results should explain *so what* per finding, not just list findings. Also the risk-label taxonomy (Personal / Safety / Spam Risk) is a clean way to triage findings.
- **Avoid:** the unrelenting severity. Four screens, zero relief, zero warmth — it converts on anxiety alone. Vanyshr's locked direction is approachable consumer fintech (Cash App reference); Cloaked is the anti-reference for tone. Also avoid gating the entire value behind "Threat level: High" theater — the verdict-first pattern can feel manipulative if the user can't verify the scan.
- **Differentiation note:** Cloaked owns "personal threat briefing." It leaves open the inverse: exposure shown with the same concreteness but a calm, competent, fix-it energy — the difference between a red alert and a dashboard. That's closer to where Vanyshr (tool-like, Self-modeled) wants to live. Note the overlap with James's framing: Cloaked's scan-and-remove loop is functionally near Serus ("closest functional competitor") but its voice is enterprise-severe; Vanyshr can take the function and leave the dread.
