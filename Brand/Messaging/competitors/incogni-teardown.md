## Identity

- **Product:** Incogni — automated personal-data removal from data brokers; recurring removals + dashboard
- **URL:** https://incogni.com/
- **Category / who it's for:** privacy-motivated consumers who want broker removal handled for them, "hassle-free"

## Positioning

- Their one-liner (quoted): "Take back control of your privacy" (`Screenshot 2026-09-09 at 9.04.25 PM.png`)
- Who they claim to serve: individuals whose info is on broker sites; the secondary headline sharpens it — "Get your privacy back. Protect yourself from stalking." (`Screenshot 2026-09-09 at 9.04.57 PM.png`)

## Voice observations

- Formality (1–5): 3 — crisp, no jargon, no warmth either. Transactional clarity.
- Playfulness (1–5): 1 — none. Every screen is an imperative or a metric.
- Confidence (1–5): 5 — the most assertive voice in the set alongside Cloaked. Verbs are seizures of agency: *take back, remove, get*.
- Notes: Incogni is the **quantified-removal machine**. Where Aura narrates and Optery explains, Incogni counts: "420+ data brokers," "2,000+ additional sites," "Total time saved 405 hrs," "Average resolution time 14 days." The dashboard is the message — progress bars and completion counts do the persuading. "Take *back*" (vs. Aura's "take control") frames privacy as stolen property being repossessed — the strongest single verb choice in the set.

## Messaging pillars (what they repeat)

1. **Restitution framing.** "Take back control of your privacy" / "Get your privacy back." Privacy was yours, taken, now returned.
2. **Scale as proof.** "Remove your personal info from 420+ data brokers" / "and from the wider web" (`Screenshot 2026-09-09 at 9.04.31 PM.png`, `...9.04.39 PM.png`); "2,000+ additional sites covered" on Unlimited.
3. **Automation without effort.** "Stay off hundreds of data broker sites, hassle-free" / "Recurring removals" / "Automated data removal."
4. **Progress you can watch.** The dashboard tour: "This is a preview of an Incogni dashboard with sample data. Once you subscribe, you'll see your real removal progress here." (`Screenshot 2026-09-09 at 9.08.22 PM.png`); "Total sent 570" / "Total time saved 405 hrs" (`Screenshot 2026-09-09 at 9.08.47 PM.png`); "Broker overview — 87 brokers covered" with Suppressed / Monitoring / In progress counts and "Broker compliance — Average resolution time 14 days" (`Screenshot 2026-09-09 at 9.09.12 PM.png`).

## Visual-verbal link

- Copy matches the design vibe: clean white/blue fintech, shield illustration, dashboard charts. Calm, competent, metric-forward — the words never emote beyond the product's own numbers. No clash. Notably *less* fear-driven than Aura/Cloaked/Guardio despite selling the same outcome.

## For Vanyshr

- **Steal:** "take *back*" — the restitution verb is the sharpest ownership framing in the category and worth workshopping against Vanyshr alternatives. The metric proof-point habit ("time saved," "average resolution time," broker counts) — Vanyshr's monitoring loop can generate the same class of numbers. The waiting-room tour ("Take a sneak peek inside Incogni while you wait") is a great pattern for any async scan.
- **Avoid:** the removal-factory narrowness — Incogni's voice has nothing to say beyond deletion counts; Vanyshr's broader tool surface (Self-modeled) needs a voice with more range. Also "Protect yourself from stalking" as a headline register — sharp, but fear-adjacent.
- **Differentiation note:** Incogni owns quantified, hands-off removal. It leaves the *investigation* voice completely open — showing the user what's out there, teaching the machinery, letting them drive. That's the Self/Serus-adjacent space James is aiming at: same category, opposite relationship to the user (operator vs. passenger).
