# ICP-Specific Objections — Vanyshr brand-messaging research

> **What this is:** the objections, hesitations, and pre-purchase questions that stop each buyer segment from converting — with verbatim quotes and sources. Feeds the brand-voice workshop and copy. · **Created:** 2026-09-24 · **Status:** draft — workshop input, not a market-sizing study.

## Method & reading guide

- Four parallel research passes (2026-09-24), one per segment cluster: survivors/DV (ICP-1), fraud victims (ICP-2), elder gifting (ICP-3), techies/creators/professions (ICP-4/5/6) + thin-evidence check on new parents/job seekers (ICP-7/8).
- Sources: NNEDV Safety Net guidance, victim-support orgs, Bogleheads/Motley Fool/MalwareTips forums, Hacker News, AgingCare caregiver forums, doxxing-victim guides, journalism safety resources, Bloomberg Law/ABA Journal, Trustpilot/BBB/ComplaintsBoard, YouTube review comments.
- **Strength ratings:** STRONG = multiple independent verbatim sources; MEDIUM = solid but fewer/inferred-from-adjacent; THIN = flagged explicitly, do not build copy on it.
- Every quote is verbatim with its source URL. Where a source is editorial/adjacent rather than a buyer's own words, it's labeled.

## ICP-1 — Harassment / stalking survivors & DV-safety situations
*Context: James will offer Vanyshr FREE via partner orgs (give-not-sell; no paid targeting of survivors). These are objections to accepting/using free help, not buying objections. Trigger validation from NNEDV Safety Net's Data Brokers guide: "your new address may have been found through data brokers, as they are easy for even people who have never heard of them to find and use" and "for a small fee, anyone can purchase more in-depth information about an individual" (mirrored at https://kaofeng-lee-s90t.squarespace.com/; https://www.techsafety.org/beingwebwise).*

### 1. "Will using this put me in more danger? Assume my device is monitored." — STRONG
- "If you think your activities are being monitored, they probably are. Abusive people are often controlling and want to know your every move. You don't need to be a computer programmer or have special skills to monitor someone's computer and Internet activities." — NRCDV, https://nrcdv.org/DV-safety-tips
- "Remember: Your computer and cell phone use can be monitored without you knowing it." — National DV Hotline, https://www.justice.gov/usao-md/page/file/1572936/dl?inline
- "Before downloading and using an app, it's important that survivors consider whether their device could be monitored by the abuser... if they think the abuser may be monitoring that device, then they should try to access that app or related information from another, safer device." — NNEDV Safety Net, *Considerations for App Developers*, https://static1.squarespace.com/static/51dc541ce4b03ebab8c5c88c/t/55f111d7e4b0c0ab25e77fe5/1441862103248/NNEDV_ConsiderationsforAppDevelopers_2015.pdf

### 2. "Will signing up or verifying create a trace my abuser can find?" — STRONG
- "Email can be intercepted like physical mail." — National DV Hotline, https://www.justice.gov/usao-md/page/file/1572936/dl?inline
- "If you are being monitored, it may be dangerous to change your computer behaviors such as suddenly deleting your entire Internet history if that is not your regular habit." — NRCDV, https://nrcdv.org/DV-safety-tips
- "When making or receiving private calls or arranging escape plans, try not to use a shared or family cell phone because cell phone billing records and phone logs might reveal your plans to an abuser." — NNEDV/Hotline tech-safety guidance, https://training.gcadv.org/Documents/ViewDocument/93

### 3. "I don't trust tech companies with my data — a breach here could get me killed." — STRONG
- "These included organizations revealing the safe addresses of the victims to their alleged abuser. In one case, a family had to be moved immediately to emergency accommodation." — The Conversation via TechXplore, https://techxplore.com/news/2023-12-domestic-violence-victim-survivors-privacy-breach.pdf
- "Complaints included instances of telcos disclosing the addresses of victim-survivors to perpetrators or of frontline staff not believing victim-survivors." — same source
- "In this field, trust is everything. Survivors need to know their information is safe and their agency is respected. Any technology that undermines trust – by leaking info, giving bad advice, or just creating confusion – should be set aside." — NNEDV Safety Net, *Survivor's Guide to AI*, https://www.techsafety.org/s/NNEDV_Safety-Net-Survivors-Guide-to-AI-m8zm.pdf

### 4. "What do you collect, how long do you keep it, who sees it — do you talk to police?" — STRONG
- "Survivors have a right to know the limitations to confidentiality and privacy before disclosing abuse." — NNEDV *Considerations for App Developers* (squarespace PDF, §ICP-1.1)
- "Be transparent about how long data on any users or their devices is retained and who may have access to that data. Does your company/organization respond to requests from law enforcement? ... Is the user notified that there has been a request for their data? Does the user control whether the information is shared?" — same
- "Privacy is extremely important to survivors of domestic violence. Many users may feel unsafe providing personal information, either about themselves or the abuser. Before asking for any identifying information, consider the purpose for why that information is collected and whether it's truly necessary." — same

### 5. "Will it actually work? Don't give me a false sense of security." — STRONG
- "When our team tested various apps, many of them did not send emergency alerts or location information as they said they would... It is extremely important that anything intended to [help] works as it says it will and does not leave the survivor with a false sense of security." — NNEDV *Considerations for App Developers*
- "Make sure that the app marketing and content does not overreach in describing what it offers." — same
- "Downloading and using apps that were created for survivors of abuse is just one thing you can do... they may not prevent or stop the abuse." — NNEDV *App Safety Considerations for Survivors*, https://www.nextstepdvproject.org/s/NNEDV-App-Considerations-for-Survivors.pdf

### 6. Partner-org vetting: "Was this built with DV experts? Tested? Simple enough in crisis?" — STRONG
- "Are you working with a reliable organization with expertise on the issue?... Any recommendations provided to a survivor from within an app should be discussed with expert organizations to ensure that the information provided doesn't inadvertently put the victim in more danger." — NNEDV *Considerations for App Developers*
- "Unfortunately, many of the apps Safety Net tested either didn't function as they promised or were complicated to navigate, set-up, and/or use... Organizations like NNEDV are available to test apps and provide guidance." — same
- Context: fewer than 1 in 5 stalking victims seek advocate support at all (SPARC via https://www.crimevictimservices.org/post/fewer-than-1-in-5-stalking-victims-seek-support-from-victim-advocates) — the referral channel is narrow and trust-gated. The referral *is* the channel; direct-to-survivor marketing is off the table.

### 7. "What's the catch? If it's free, am I the product?" — MEDIUM (survivor-voice layer thin; adjacent evidence strong)
- "An online data broker that charged consumers $10 based on the promise that it could 'lock their records' so others could not see or buy them, has agreed to settle Federal Trade Commission charges that its claims were deceptive and violated federal law." — FTC, https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2010/09/online-data-broker-settles-ftc-charges-privacy-pledges-were-deceptive
- "So-called 'free' services can only exist by monetising their data, and selling advertising. They are not free. People are paying for these services with their personal data, and ultimately their privacy." — TechSmart, http://www.techsmart.co.za/business/Selling-your-privacy-for-free-services.html
- "Be upfront at the beginning of each conversation about sharing information and the risks that may be involved." — Ohio DV Network, https://www.odvn.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/TechSafety_Pamphlet.pdf

### 8. "How discreet is this? No app icon, emails, or calls that give me away." — MEDIUM
- "Icon Options: Some apps have been built to look like something else on the phone. Examples include apps that look like calculators..." — NNEDV *Considerations for App Developers*
- "An app requiring a password could gain the attention of and be suspicious to an abuser... it's recommended that passwords be an option that survivors choose to use but not a default." — same
- "Click the red 'X' in the upper-right corner or 'Escape' button on your keyboard twice at any time to leave TheHotline.org immediately." — thehotline.org, https://www.thehotline.org/resources/stalking-safety-planning/
- "Be sure to check with survivors when it is safe to call and whether it is safe to leave a message." — ODVN pamphlet (URL in §ICP-1.7)

### 9. "Could the removal process itself expose me — confirmation emails, verification calls?" — MEDIUM
- "Note that some opt-out forms paradoxically require you to submit personal data in order to have it removed; be your own judge of whether this is wise." — Computerworld, https://www.computerworld.com/article/1618134/doxxing-defense-remove-your-personal-info-from-data-brokers-2.html
- "You need to provide an email address to receive the confirmation link... You'll get a second email once the process is complete." (Spokeo) — CSO, https://www.csoonline.com/article/560319/how-to-scrub-your-private-data-from-people-finder-sites.html
- "You need a phone number in order to be able to opt out." (Whitepages) — freeCodeCamp, https://www.freecodecamp.org/news/white-pages-removal-remove-information-spokeo-peoplefinder-whitepages-opt-out/

### 10. "This won't fix the real sources — courts, voter rolls, public records." — MEDIUM
- "Some court systems publish records online, which could contain compromising personal information like names or addresses." — National DV Hotline
- "Removing information from data brokers doesn't delete the original records the data brokers used to get [it]." — NNEDV Data Brokers guide (squarespace mirror, §ICP-1 intro)
- NNEDV itself routes survivors to state Address Confidentiality Programs (https://www.techsafety.org/beingwebwise) — the honest positioning is "one layer," plus ACP referral.

### 11. "Could my abuser use this service too — against me or someone else?" — THIN
- "As much as a service like that would be useful, I am skeptical on how to set it up without it being abused. Abusers are likely to put their exes in there as revenge as well." — Medium commenter, https://medium.com/@drredoctopus/as-much-as-a-service-like-that-would-be-useful-i-am-skeptical-on-how-to-set-it-up-without-it-being-9cda98962174 (single anecdotal source, about a different service type — flagged thin)

### 12. Evidence gap — first-person survivor voices: THIN (stated explicitly)
Targeted searches for first-person survivor accounts of data-broker exposure/removal (Reddit r/domesticviolence, r/Stalking, survivor forums) did not return citable threads. The strong evidence base is advocate/org guidance — which in this field is the operative voice (advocates aggregate survivor experience). Do not brief as if survivor-voice evidence exists.

**Discreetness requirements for product/onboarding (synthesized from the above):** assume a monitored device (advocate-provisioned signup on a shelter/safe device as the primary flow); no unexpected outreach ever; neutral sender names/subject lines/$0 descriptors; quick-exit pattern on any survivor-facing surface; minimal PII stated plainly with retention/access/law-enforcement policy *before* disclosure; agent-handled broker verification via provisioned aliases (a genuine differentiator to name to advocates); no overclaiming ("reduces broker-listing exposure," never "your abuser can't find you"); name uncovered layers + ACP referrals; partner-org expert review before any shelter/hotline recommends it; dual-use guard (advocate-initiated/verified requests).

## ICP-2 — Identity-theft & fraud victims / the breach-fatigued

### 1. "I already did the free stuff — freeze + bank alerts is the real protection." — STRONG
- "Lifelock or credit monitoring is like viewing the camera after your car and all your possessions have been trucked off. Useless." — Bogleheads (CAsage), https://www.bogleheads.org/forum/viewtopic.php?p=6746500&sid=b81face8d01d6ac5aa9fdbeedd1d3a3d
- "Yes, you need to FREEZE your credit at the 3 major bureaus... it's the gold standard in protection." — same thread
- "Besides freezing my credit at Equifax, TransUnion and Experion, I also selected the bank option to get a text every time my VISA card is charged. All for free." — Bogleheads (NYCaviator), https://www.bogleheads.org/forum/viewtopic.php?p=6307480

### 2. "Why hand all my sensitive info to another company that could get breached?" — STRONG
- "So do you think this kind of service is worthy paying for or is it just more headache as those services need all your info to locate your data online, and are not immune to being hacked themselves?" — MalwareTips, https://malwaretips.com/threads/are-online-data-removal-services-worthy-paying-for.132682/
- "Finally, how much do you trust yet another company to keep your data safe? After all you are handing over your PII to some other faction so they can use it to remove your PII from someone else's database whilst populating their own?" — same thread
- "DeleteMe has no policy against storing or not selling the data you give it access to" — Instagram comment on sponsored DeleteMe reel, https://www.instagram.com/reel/DYfNNVZOkBh/
- Institutional weight: FTC's $100M LifeLock settlement (deceptive security claims) and KrebsOnSecurity's March 2024 finding that OneRep's CEO founded dozens of people-search sites — which led Mozilla to drop OneRep and shut down Monitor Plus (Dec 17, 2025). Trust is the entire sale.

### 3. "Isn't it already too late? My data is out there. Barn door, horse fled." — STRONG
- "You are just closing the barn door after the horse has fled." — Motley Fool victim of a $900,000 ACATS theft, https://discussion.fool.com/t/ot-how-to-erase-your-info-from-internet/115741
- "While removing your data from data broker sites can help protect your privacy, it won't protect you from the numerous ways in which scammers and identity thieves use information that has already been exposed." — Aura (competitor page, honest concession), https://www.aura.com/learn/data-removal-vs-identity-theft
- "I've had my identity stolen twice. Both times, what caught it wasn't a service that scrubbed my name off some site — it was one that warned me before the damage was done." — reviewer in a DeleteMe-alternatives video, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Sr8zVx9pYFU
- Beatable: "I have been dealing with identity theft for over 25 years. Finding delete me and having 99% of me removed from the internet is the best thing I EVER could have done" — SmartCustomer review (Kevin H.), https://www.smartcustomer.com/reviews/joindeleteme.com?page=13

### 4. "The useful parts are free or DIY." — MEDIUM-STRONG
- "99% of the things that the company does can be done for free by you. However what it takes is time...how valuable is your time? I did it but it took me 5 hours." — MalwareTips, https://malwaretips.com/threads/are-online-data-removal-services-worthy-paying-for.132682/
- "so absurd. why is it $129/year to remove myself from lists that I didn't consent to be added to in the first place?" — Hacker News, https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=24837200
- Counterweight: "How much is your time worth? Doing it manually is a pain in the ass and takes a lot of time. Deleteme is not perfect, but they do a pretty good job. Customer since 2010" — HN (same thread)

### 5. "My bank eats the fraud anyway — what am I paying to prevent?" — MEDIUM (direct victim voice tying this to *removal* is thin)
- "when someone steals your plastic, federal laws limit your loss liability to $50 per stolen card... Card issuers and payment processors also typically cover customers with zero liability policies" — Consumer Reports, https://www.consumerreports.org/cro/news/2014/03/pricey-identity-theft-protection-is-not-worth-the-cost/index.htm
- "94 percent of credit-card-fraud victims lost no money out of pocket" — same source
- "Out-of-pocket losses tend to be low because credit card companies and banks already provide fraud protection guarantees for free." — Nasdaq, https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/what-is-identity-theft-insurance-and-should-you-get-it-2019-10-12

### 6. "How fast does this work — and what protects me while removals are pending?" — MEDIUM
- "They only remove your information every 3 months. If I'm paying $129/year I want the removals to be submitted daily." — Hacker News, https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=24837200
- "if a broker republishes your data in month two, you wait until the next quarter." — SecurityHero, https://securityhero.io/us/news/what-data-removal-services-do-delete-me-and-can-you-do-it-yourself/
- "At the time of this writing, the removal request with the broker that has my information is still ongoing." — ZDNET review of DeleteMe

### 7. "I don't believe the pitch — I'm breach-fatigued and the category smells like sponsored fear." — MEDIUM
- "fewer than one in ten consumers who have had personal information exposed in a major data breach take advantage of credit monitoring services offered by the company responsible for the breach – evidence of what the company says is data breach fatigue." — Ponemon/Experian research summary
- "If I had a dollar for every time I got a notification somewhere that some information or the other of mine had potentially been compromised....." — Bogleheads (Threetrueoutcomes), https://www.bogleheads.org/forum/viewtopic.php?t=459510&sid=228774ab16d84a5b802acf7ec097f1da&view=print
- "So funny how you smile through the whole 'your info is not safe' monologue" / "I prefer your content without hawking a product" — Instagram comments on sponsored DeleteMe reel, https://www.instagram.com/reel/DYfNNVZOkBh/

### 8. "Will removing Whitepages/Spokeo data actually reduce fraud — or just spam?" — MEDIUM
- Victims credit detection to transaction/credit monitoring, not people-search cleanup; Consumer Reports testing found paid services removed only 35% of 332 profiles after four months vs. 70% manual in a week (TechSpot), https://www.techspot.com/community/topics/paid-services-that-remove-you-from-people-finder-sites-arent-very-effective.287366/

### 9. "Who do fraud victims actually trust for recommendations?" — THIN (research gap)
Direct evidence on the trusted-recommendation hierarchy for data removal specifically is thin. What sources show: post-fraud the first call is the bank/fraud department → credit bureaus → IdentityTheft.gov (FTC) → police; peer personal-finance forums function as trusted advice venues; KrebsOnSecurity is the watchdog trust anchor; creator sponsorships draw "hawking a product" backlash. Hypotheses to test: bank-fraud-department referrals, FTC-aligned guidance, published independent-test methodology.

## ICP-3 — Adult children buying scam protection for elderly parents
*Gift/purchase-on-behalf motion: the adult child pays, the parent is enrolled.*

### 1. "I can't enroll them without them" — the parent MUST participate in signup — STRONG
- "Simply verify your identity using the email sent to the email address you entered, and you will be good to go." (i.e., the parent must access their own email) — https://beebom.com/delete-digital-footprint-incogni/

- "Delete Me also required a photo of a driver's license or passport, which made the speaker uncomfortable." — https://summarize.tech
- "You will need him with you when making these phone calls so he can tell them it's OK to talk to you (unless you have POA and it's already in effect)." — AgingCare forum, https://www.agingcare.com/questions/any-resources-available-out-there-that-can-help-with-the-elderly-that-have-been-taken-advantage-of-b-483712.htm?orderby=oldest

### 2. The parent refuses — autonomy, privacy, dignity — STRONG
- "My mom protested the entire time that she could do it... there was no way. She couldn't even remember from visit to visit why I was doing all this work." — AgingCare forum (same thread)
- "talking with a parent about money can be difficult because it's most always a privacy issue... 'Resistance is common,' he said." — George Kiraly, CFP, "When Older Folks Refuse Help – But Need It," https://36a23fde-a309-470b-a979-089d3e4db1a01e30cdf18a78e1.pdf

### 3. "Is THIS a scam targeting old people?" — the meta-objection — STRONG
- "Incogni requires you to provide them with your real information like your full name, current and past physical addresses, phone numbers, and dates of birth. Critics argue that gathering your real identity into one corporate database creates a new security risk..." — https://medium.com/@iqramustafa668/is-incogni-legit-or-a-scam-does-it-actually-remove-your-data-41eef747f508

- "Expect recovery scammers who claim they can retrieve the money for a fee." — https://getoutofdebt.org/252399/elderly-parent-phone-scam-charged-what-to-do

### 4. "Why pay when the real protections are free?" — MEDIUM
- "You don't know what info your dad shared. Put a freeze on his credit, and report possible identity theft on the Federal Trade Commission website." — AgingCare forum (same thread as §ICP-3.1)
- "If budget is limited, start with PrivacyOn's free scan plus a free credit freeze at all three bureaus; that pairing removes more real-world risk per dollar than any monitoring bundle." — https://www.privacyon.com/blog/does-lifelock-remove-your-information-from-the-internet
- "You can contact data brokers directly and request removal. It's free and it's your legal right. What it isn't is quick or simple." — https://amac.us/scams/your-information-is-already-out-there-here-s-what-it-takes-to-get-it-back

### 5. No real "gift" flow — family plans still require the parent to self-enroll — MEDIUM
No major competitor offers a one-click gift checkout. Incogni's Family & Friends plan forces the buyer to send an invitation link the parent must accept within 24 hours, creating their own account and entering their own PII.
- "Share the generated link with your family member or friend via email, SMS, or a messaging app. The links are valid for 24 hours from the moment you click 'Create Link.'" — https://cyberguy.com/privacy/take-back-privacy-incogni-erase-digital-footprint/

- Notable: Aura is the only competitor with a senior-framed benefit — "Senior Expense Reimbursement — Covers fraud against your senior family member" on the $5M Family plan ($25/mo billed annually) — https://www.aura.com/learn/identity-theft-insurance-coverage

### 6. "Who has the authority?" when the parent is cognitively declining — MEDIUM

As he is NOT, I assume, thusly incompetent... There is no way you can protect him." — AgingCare forum (AlvaDeer), same thread as §ICP-3.1

### 7. Ongoing participation the parent can't sustain (tech literacy) — MEDIUM-THIN (failure mode inferred)
- "Many sites require email or phone confirmation. Prepare for an inbox full of 'Are you sure?' messages and the occasional confusing phone verification." — https://cyberinsider.com/data-removal/best-data-removal-services/
- "She couldn't even remember from visit to visit why I was doing all this work." — AgingCare forum (same thread as §ICP-3.1)
- No source directly documents a parent *failing* a verification step; inferred from documented verification requirements + documented cognitive/engagement limits in the same population.

### 8. "Will this actually stop the scams?" — THIN-MEDIUM
- "It's worth being honest about what it can and cannot do. Incogni won't scrub every last corner of the internet, and no service that respects you would promise that the scam calls will stop overnight." — https://nationalcybersecurity.com
- "My mom would just reflexively run to answer the phone any time that it rang... Isolation from phone, computer and mail is the only thing that works." — AgingCare forum (Missymiss), same thread as §ICP-3.1
- Counter-evidence (the segment's anchor): "More importantly, the scam calls and messages stopped. She is no longer being targeted by scammers... I'm pretty confident that she will no longer be targeted by scammers, which gives me great peace of mind." — https://www.cultofmac.com/reviews/incogni-review (affiliate-disclosed)

## ICP-4 — Privacy-pragmatist techies / DIY graduates

### 1. The privacy paradox — "you want my PII to protect my PII" — STRONG
- "I almost went through with DeleteMe, but it felt paradoxical to give all my info to one company so they could remove it from others. I understand they need it to do the work but it didn't feel right. They requested photo ID, SSN, all past addresses, online handles, family member information, etc. It was just too much." — HN, https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=38990755
- "That said, they could very easily have a data breach and every customers full info would then be out in the wild. Were not talking about ordinary payment details either, just full on dox - every address you have lived at, your license scan, all emails, phone numbers, its crazy." — HN (same thread)
- "how much do you trust yet another company to keep your data safe? After all you are handing over your PII to some other faction so they can use it to remove your PII from someone else's database whilst populating their own?" — MalwareTips, https://malwaretips.com/threads/are-online-data-removal-services-worthy-paying-for.132682/

### 2. "I can do this myself — for free" — STRONG
- "99% of the things that the company does can be done for free by you. However what it takes is time...how valuable is your time? I did it but it took me 5 hours." — MalwareTips (same thread)
- "The quickest, most effective, and most private way to remove yourself from people search sites is to submit opt-out requests manually to each site." — PrivacyGuides, https://github.com/privacyguides/privacyguides.org/blob/HEAD/docs/data-broker-removals.md
- "2026 DIY Opt-Out Manual For Removal From Over 400 Sites. The guide shows difficulty rating, an estimate of how long it will take, and the exact instructions to opt-out per site." — thumpersecure/opt-out-manual-2026, https://github.com/thumpersecure/opt-out-manual-2026

### 3. Proof demands — "show me it worked" — STRONG
- "I have never seen this claim ever backed up with evidence. How do I KNOW that these data deletion services DO anything?... If someone could show me any proof I would buy such a service." — Techlore Forums, https://discuss.techlore.tech/t/data-deletion-services-whats-the-point/6730
- "I don't use blind trust. I trust based on evidence. Right now I don't see a mustard seed of evidence." — Techlore (same thread)
- "Consumer Reports has noted that the effectiveness of removal services varies significantly, and that users should verify results independently rather than relying solely on the service's own reporting." — webpronews, https://www.webpronews.com/the-invisible-threat-to-your-family-why-data-brokers-know-more-about-you-than-you-think-and-what-to-do-about-it/

### 4. "Services are measurably worse than DIY" (Consumer Reports study) — STRONG
- "Manual opt-outs were more effective than people search removal services and had quicker results, with 70% gone within a week, compared with anywhere from 0% to 58.7% removed in the first week via the people-search removal services." — CR Advocacy, https://advocacy.consumerreports.org/press_release/consumer-reports-evaluation-of-people-search-site-removal-services-finds-that-they-are-largely-ineffective
- "Without exception, information about each participant still appeared on some of the 13 people-search sites at the one-week, one-month, and four-month intervals." — CR Advocacy (same page)
- Vendor rebuttal (mark source): Optery notes the study "did not have regular consumers performing the opt-outs. Instead, it had highly trained data privacy professional(s) performing the opt-outs." — https://www.optery.com/optery-statement-on-consumer-reports-people-search-removal-study/

## Cross-ICP objection taxonomy

### Universal objections (hit 4+ segments — answer these on core pages)

**U1. The privacy paradox — "why should I give you my PII to protect my PII?"**
Strongest cross-cutting objection in the set. Hits ICP-1 (#3, #4 — with life-or-death stakes), ICP-2 (#2 — LifeLock/OneRep institutional weight), ICP-3 (#3 — the meta-scam fear), ICP-4 (#1 — the most-repeated HN objection). The category's original sin: every competitor collects the dossier to delete the dossier.

**U2. Proof demands — "how do I know it worked, independently of your dashboard?"**
Hits ICP-2 (#8), ICP-4 (#3, #4 — CR study), ICP-5 (scope honesty), ICP-6 (#1 — the judges study). Incogni's broker-confirmation-as-completed, Optery's not-founds-as-removals, DeleteMe's quarterly PDFs — the category taught buyers that counters lie.

**U3. The efficacy ceiling — "isn't it too late / won't it just come back?"**
Hits ICP-1 (#10 — courts/voter rolls), ICP-2 (#3 — barn door), ICP-4 (#8 — the long tail), ICP-5 (#2 — scope gaps), ICP-6 (#1 — even the judiciary program failed), plus the category-wide #1 anger theme (data reappears). Two flavors: *already-out-there* (SSN circulated) and *comes-back* (relisting).

**U4. Free alternatives exist — "why pay for this?"**
Hits ICP-2 (#1 — freeze is free and gold-standard; #4 — DIY), ICP-3 (#4 — freeze + FTC), ICP-4 (#2 — GitHub 400-site guide). The freeze is the anchor: free, government-adjacent, and genuinely effective at its job.

**U5. Category marketing distrust — "every review is sponsored, every stat is inflated"**
Hits ICP-2 (#7 — breach fatigue, "hawking a product"), ICP-3 (#3 — affiliate baggage), ICP-4 (#6 — affiliate-link rage), ICP-6 (#1 — vendor-audited program failed). Buyers arrive pre-inoculated against claims.

**U6. Speed and timeline — "what protects me while I wait?"**
Hits ICP-2 (#6 — quarterly cadence), ICP-4 (#7 — "submitted daily"), ICP-5 (#1 — doxxing is an emergency). The category's 3–6-month timelines collide with urgent triggers.

### Segment-specific objections (answer on segment surfaces, not core pages)

- **Monitored-device / trace-creation fears** (ICP-1 only — #1, #2, #8, #9): onboarding must assume surveillance. No other segment thinks this way.
- **Free-help skepticism + partner-org vetting** (ICP-1 only — #6, #7): "what's the catch" and NNEDV-grade expert review. Unique to the give-not-sell motion.
- **False-safety dread** (ICP-1 #5): overclaiming isn't just a trust issue here, it's a physical-safety issue. Bounded promises are load-bearing.
- **Parent cooperation / autonomy / dignity** (ICP-3 only — #1, #2, #6, #7): the buyer can't complete enrollment alone, and the enrollee may actively resist. No competitor has solved this; it's a product gap, not just a copy gap.
- **No true gift flow** (ICP-3 #5): invitation links expiring in 24 hours, parents entering their own PII — the purchase-on-behalf motion is unbuilt category-wide. Genuine wedge.
- **Event-vs-subscription mismatch** (ICP-5 #3): doxxing is an incident; subscriptions are maintenance. Needs an incident-response story distinct from the maintenance story.
- **Streisand / attention risk** (ICP-5 #4): removal activity itself as a signal. Pre-incident hygiene framing is the answer.
- **DIY cost math + open-source alternatives** (ICP-4 #2, #5): this segment will price your $8/mo against a GitHub repo and a Saturday. Time-arbitrage + persistence is the only honest counter — never coverage magic.
- **"The bank eats the fraud anyway"** (ICP-2 #5): sell what banks don't cover — cleanup hours, disruption, recurrence anxiety — not fraud dollars.
- **Efficacy-ceiling-by-precedent** (ICP-6 #1): the judges study is the proof-demand objection with a body count. This segment needs the most restrained claims in the portfolio.
- **Employer-paid question** (ICP-6 #2): vendor-pushed, buyer-unproven. Hypothesis, not a message.

## Copy must answer — the questions, not the answers

For each top objection, the question Vanyshr's copy has to resolve. Framed as questions deliberately — the workshop writes the answers.

**Universal (core pages):**
1. "Why should I give you my personal data to protect my personal data?" → *Copy must answer: exactly what is collected, why each field is needed, how long it's kept, who can see it, what happens to it if I cancel — and what you fundamentally cannot do with it (inability-framing).*
2. "How do I know it actually worked — without taking your word for it?" → *Copy must answer: what independent verification looks like (before/after, per-listing timestamps, re-checks) — and what you refuse to count.*
3. "My data's already out there / it'll just come back — so what's the point?" → *Copy must answer: what removal can and can't do about already-circulating data, what happens on relisting (re-removal as the product, not the caveat), and where synthetic obfuscation changes the math.*
4. "I can freeze my credit for free / do opt-outs myself — why pay you?" → *Copy must answer: where Vanyshr sits in the layered defense (never as a freeze replacement), and what the buyer is actually buying — follow-through, recurrence handling, verification, time.*
5. "Why should I believe any of this when every review is sponsored?" → *Copy must answer: with checkable proof, published methodology, and restraint — the credibility has to be structural, not claimed.*
6. "How fast does this work, and what do I do on day one?" → *Copy must answer: the honest timeline per listing, the day-one action list (freeze, alerts — the things that aren't Vanyshr), and per-listing progress with timestamps instead of a big counter.*

**Survivors (partner-org surfaces — never paid targeting):**
7. "Will using this create a trace my abuser can find? Assume my device is monitored." → *Copy/product must answer: the advocate-provisioned flow, zero-survivor-device requirement, no unexpected outreach — before asking for anything.*
8. "It's free — what's the catch? Am I the product?" → *Copy must answer: the funding model in one plain sentence, stated before signup, in language a shelter advocate can repeat.*
9. "Will this actually work, or will it give me a false sense of safety?" → *Copy must answer: bounded, checkable claims ("reduces broker-listing exposure") plus named uncovered layers and ACP referrals — never "your abuser can't find you."*
10. "What do you keep, who sees it, do you talk to police?" → *Copy must answer: retention periods, access lists, and the law-enforcement-request policy in plain language, before any disclosure.*

**Elder gifting (gift surfaces):**
11. "Can I do this for my parent without making them do homework or surrender their dignity?" → *Copy must answer: exactly what the parent has to do (ideally near-zero), what the buyer handles, and how consent is obtained respectfully.*
12. "How do I know this isn't another scam targeting old people?" → *Copy must answer: with legitimacy signals that aren't affiliate reviews — and an honest statement of what removal can and can't do about scam calls.*

**Techies (comparison/FAQ surfaces):**
13. "Prove it — independently verifiable, not your own counter." → *Copy must answer: with methodology a skeptic can replicate (self-check instructions included).*
14. "Why is this better than the GitHub guide and a Saturday?" → *Copy must answer: with time-arbitrage honesty and the persistence story (relisting never sleeps) — never coverage magic.*

**Creators (incident-response surfaces):**
15. "I need it gone this week — what's the actual incident timeline?" → *Copy must answer: the fast-first-pass story, separated from the maintenance story; and what to do in the first 24 hours that isn't Vanyshr.*
16. "What can't you remove? Say it upfront." → *Copy must answer: the scope gaps (Google vs. Bing, voter rolls, public records) before purchase, not after.*

**High-risk professions (professional surfaces):**
17. "The judiciary's own removal program failed — why would this work?" → *Copy must answer: with the most restrained claims in the portfolio; acknowledge the ceiling, sell the floor.*
18. "Can my employer provide this?" → *Copy must answer: whether a benefits/HR path exists — currently a hypothesis, not a message.*

**Fraud victims (post-breach surfaces):**
19. "I froze my credit — what does this add?" → *Copy must answer: the layered-defense slot (freeze blocks new credit; removal shrinks the targeting/impersonation surface) — never as a freeze replacement.*
20. "Will this stop fraud, or just spam? Be precise." → *Copy must answer: the honest split — exposed SSNs can't be un-breached; current addresses/phones/profile linkage still drive targeted phishing — and where synthetic data changes the "too late" math.*
