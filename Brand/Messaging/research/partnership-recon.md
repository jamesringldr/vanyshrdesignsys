# Partnership / affiliate recon — Vanyshr

> **What this is:** ranked affiliate, referral, and white-label partnership opportunities for Vanyshr (not enterprise B2B sales), with evidence, terms, and solo-founder approach angles. · **Created:** 2026-09-24 · **Status:** research complete — outreach not started.

## TL;DR — ranked by alignment × solo-founder accessibility

1. **Real-estate associations (member benefit)** — proven playbook (FOREWARN), named channel, unoccupied for data removal, local first door.
2. **Independent financial advisors / RIAs (client perk)** — category actively buying this in 2025–26 (Carefull deals), co-branded perk model, one-person closable.
3. **DV/survivor orgs (free-tier referral, mission channel)** — Ring×NNEDV template exists; no data-removal vendor there yet; give-not-sell.
4. **Divorce & family law (reciprocal referral)** — strong need, Kentucky precedent, ethics-constrained deal shape, firm-by-firm sales.
5. **Vanyshr's own affiliate program** — category rate card is public; launch from day one.
6. **Employer benefits (small-business / PEO direct)** — proven channel, quoted pricing, but broker-gated; small-biz is the solo path.
7. **Creator orgs (CGA)** — open door, young nonprofit; cheap parallel bet, no perk-buying precedent.
8. **VPN / password-manager bundling** — zero third-party precedent (Incogni is in-family); useful as rate intelligence, not an outreach target.
9. **Cyber-insurance carriers** — right economics, wrong deal shape for a solo founder; 2027 channel.
10. **White-label API (Optery model)** — proven, but demands SOC 2 / SLAs; later stage.

## 1. Real-estate associations — member benefit (rank #1)

**Why it aligns:** Agents' names, faces, and phone numbers are public by profession; many state license-lookup databases publish home addresses. Agents meet strangers alone in empty properties. The industry already spends on personal safety as a budget line. The pitch is crisp: "FOREWARN vets the client before the showing; Vanyshr removes *your* address from the people-search sites so a disgruntled lead can't find your home."

**Evidence (with URLs):**
- **FOREWARN × dozens of REALTOR associations** — the exact playbook: agent-safety services provided *at no additional cost to individual agents*, funded as a member benefit. Examples: Buffalo Niagara Association of REALTORS® (3,600+ members), nine North Carolina associations, New Hampshire Association of REALTORS® (6,500), Northern Virginia Association of REALTORS® (13,000), Northwest Arkansas Board of REALTORS® (3,200). https://lifestyle.myeaglecountry.com/story/127634/forewarn-to-provide-identity-verification-services-to-buffalo-niagara-association-of-realtors-to-promote-agent-safety/ · https://www.globenewswire.com/en/news-release/2022/09/29/2525131/0/en/FOREWARN-Partners-with-Nine-North-Carolina-REALTOR-Associations.html
- **NAR REALTOR Benefits®** — the national partnership channel: "30-plus industry-leading partners," managed by a VP of strategic alliances. A named partnerships desk exists — a door with a name on it. https://www.rismedia.com/2025/06/24/nar-realtor-benefits-announces-hp-as-newest-partner/
- **NAR 2024 Member Safety data + explicit data-broker-removal guidance:** 22% of residential Realtors reported fearing for personal safety or personal information (25% of women); industry safety guides now instruct agents to "search your own name and city, and request removal from at least one data-broker site." https://www.stewart.com/en/insights/real-estate-agent-safety-for-personal-protection-and-cybersecurity
- **Press framing (Inman, Sept 2026):** "No One Is Protecting Real Estate Agents. That's A Problem." https://www.inman.com/2026/09/18/protecting-real-estate-agents/
- **Honest gap:** No data-removal/privacy company is partnered with NAR, any state association, or a major brokerage as a member benefit. FOREWARN (client *verification*) is the only adjacent motion. The *agent-side* privacy channel appears unoccupied.

**White-label feasibility:** Very high, and already modeled by FOREWARN: "Agent Privacy Shield, powered by Vanyshr," sold to associations as a per-member or flat member benefit; the association pays, agents get it free. PureVPN also runs an explicit white-label data-broker-removal product for B2B partners ("a partner keeps the billing relationship, the support relationship, and the renewal data instead of handing that value to an outside brand"). https://www.purevpn.com/white-label/data-broker-registration-requirements-by-state/

**Approach angle (solo founder):** Don't start with KW/Compass corporate. Start with the **Kansas City Regional Association of REALTORS®** (home market) or any local association with a reachable exec. Pitch: "FOREWARN protects your agents at the showing; nothing protects their families when the showing goes wrong — a flat member benefit that scrubs every agent's personal data." One signed association = a referenceable pilot for NAR REALTOR Benefits® and neighboring associations.

## 3. DV / stalking survivor orgs — free-tier referral, mission channel (rank #3)

**Why it aligns:** A people-search listing with a home address is a physical-safety threat, not an annoyance. James's instinct is right: marketing *to* survivors is icky; **donating product seats through trusted orgs** is not. There is a live, proven template for exactly this — and no data-removal vendor occupies it.

**Evidence (with URLs):**
- **The template — Ring × NNEDV × TechSoup (give-not-sell, at scale):** 35,000+ devices donated to survivors over three years, plus 10,000 more in year four, with a free Ring Protect Plan for the life of each device; devices go *directly to verified survivor-serving organizations*, distributed *case by case by advocates* as part of safety planning; TechSoup validates eligibility and handles distribution; Safety Net built advocate training resources. https://www.techsafety.org/blog/2025/10/15/ring-and-nnedv-complete-a-3rd-year-of-donations
- **NNEDV takes corporate tech partnerships:** its Safety Net page thanks "Amazon/Ring, Gen Digital/Norton, Google, Meta, and Uber for their Technology Safety support and partnership." https://nnedv.org/content/technology-safety/ — NNEDV's 2022 Tech Summit sponsors: Google, NortonLifeLock, Uber (presenting); Meta, Match Group, Apple, Airbnb, Kaspersky. https://nnedv.org/content/virtual-technology-summit-2022/
- **Direct precedent — Kentucky Secretary of State Safe at Home × DeleteMe (Oct 2024):** eligible DV survivors get DeleteMe's removal service; ~15 of 125 members signed up immediately; reported **~800 listings removed (~51/person)** and **~4,500 pieces of PII removed (~322/person)**. https://kentuckylantern.com/2024/10/16/kentucky-domestic-violence-survivors-now-have-a-way-to-remove-personal-information-from-online/
- **Survivor orgs actively sourcing tools:** Safe House Project partnered with Protect Privacy AI (image-abuse detection/removal) — free to survivors and orgs during beta, introduced via a 30-minute org briefing. https://www.linkedin.com/posts/brittany-dunn-mba_for-years-when-a-survivor-told-us-that-images-activity-7494041293300449280-SPNs
- **Honest gap:** No evidence that NNEDV, RAINN, VictimConnect/NCVC, the Cyber Civil Rights Initiative, or state DV coalitions currently refer or partner with a *commercial data-broker removal* tool. Kentucky's DeleteMe deal is the only survivor-program → data-removal referral verified. The channel is real but wide open.

**What these orgs actually need from a partner (non-extractive approach):**
1. **Copy the Ring structure, not a referral link:** donate seats via an intermediary (TechSoup-style) to verified orgs; *advocates* decide who gets it, case by case. Never market to survivors directly. Never harvest survivor data.
2. **Lead with a privacy proof, not a pitch deck.** Orgs will ask "are you just another data broker?" A published data-minimization policy + third-party verification answers it (Incogni's Deloitte audit is the category's trust currency).
3. **Bring advocate enablement, not just logins:** a one-page advocate guide (what removal does/doesn't do, limits, safety caveats — e.g., removal requests can *confirm* identity to a broker, the catch-22 Optery discloses).
4. **Trauma-informed product basics:** no dark-pattern upsells in the survivor tier, easy account deletion, no marketing email, an obvious exit path.
5. **Frame the org as a design partner** (paid advisory, not free labor); credit them publicly only with permission.

**Approach angle (solo founder):** (a) **NNEDV Safety Net / techsafety.org** — the literal intake point for tech-safety corporate partnerships; pitch = "Ring, but for your data-broker footprint": X donated seats/yr for advocates to allocate. (b) **State Safe-at-Home / address-confidentiality programs** — Kentucky's is the proof case; one state office, one decision-maker, measurable outcomes. (c) **Safe House Project-style orgs** — small, fast-moving, good for a pilot that generates the case study.

## 4. Divorce & family law — reciprocal referral (rank #4)

**Why it aligns:** Divorce/custody clients face acute, safety-driven privacy needs — a stalker ex can find a new address via people-search sites in minutes. Attorneys have a natural advisory moment (the intake consult) to recommend privacy protection as part of client safety planning. Firms already sell on "discretion/confidentiality," so a privacy service fits their value story. Vanyshr's "synthetic" obfuscation is a sharp hook (poisoning the well so an ex can't trust what they find).

**Evidence (with URLs):**
- **Kentucky Safe at Home × DeleteMe** (same precedent as §3) — proves an institutional buyer already pays a data-removal vendor for exactly the stalker-ex scenario. https://kentuckylantern.com/2024/10/16/kentucky-domestic-violence-survivors-now-have-a-way-to-remove-personal-information-from-online/
- **Divorce Navigator partner program:** offers family-law firms a branded referral page ("Your firm name and welcome message on a dedicated signup page… free, within 24 hours") — family-law firms adopt partner/referral SaaS tools. https://app.divorce-plan.com/partners/one-pager
- **OurFamilyWizard:** co-parenting app distributed *through* family-law professionals, with free practitioner accounts for mediators/GALs/evaluators — the established model of a non-legal product sold via family-law professionals. https://www.ourfamilywizard.ca/node/12334
- **Honest gap:** No evidence of any data-removal company currently partnering with family-law firms or bar associations. Unoccupied channel.

**Ethics constraints (the hard rule):** Attorneys cannot share legal fees with nonlawyers (ABA Model Rule 5.4(a)) and generally cannot give anything of value for recommending the lawyer's services (Rule 7.2(b)). The safe harbor: Rule 7.2(b)(4) permits reciprocal referrals to a nonlawyer professional **if (i) the agreement is not exclusive, and (ii) the client is informed of its existence and nature**. NYSBA Opinion 1116 (2017) clarifies the test: payment tied to the value of the vendor's services (not the lawyer's fees) is the clean structure. https://qa.americanbar.org/content/dam/aba/administrative/professional_responsibility/mrpc_migrated/ethics_8_02.pdf · https://nysba.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/Opn-1116-doc.pdf

**White-label feasibility:** High. Vanyshr as a branded "Client Safety Check" in the firm's intake package, or a bar-association member benefit. Onerep shows the motion at scale: its tech "works behind the scenes to power privacy features offered by globally recognized brands." https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/01/30/3018351/0/en/Onerep-Honors-Pledge-to-Educate-Consumers-on-How-to-Manage-Their-Data-and-Warns-Against-Using-Third-Parties-for-Removal-from-Non-Public-Data-Brokers.html

**Approach angle (solo founder):** Lead with the **reciprocal-referral structure** (non-exclusive, client-disclosed) — no referral fees tied to case fees; either a flat service-value-based arrangement or pure mutual referral. Knock on doors in this order: (1) local family-law boutiques + local bar association family-law sections (bring the Kentucky one-pager: "your state already funds this for DV survivors — your clients have the same exposure"); (2) DV shelters/coalitions as mission partners; (3) eventually state DV programs replicating Kentucky's model.

- **Allstate Scam Protection** distributed *exclusively through workplace benefits* to ~7M employees (Nov 2025). https://www.stocktitan.net/news/ALL/allstate-launches-scam-protection-to-safeguard-workers-finances-from-euqs25i0l2s8.html
- **LifeLock Benefit Elite:** "created specifically for employers and brokers," broker-distributed, payroll-deduct pricing ($8.49–$23.36/mo tiers). https://www.napa-net.org/news-info/daily-news/identity-theft-protection-401ks

The DeleteMe-for-Business precedent proves removal-only sells into this channel; Vanyshr's agents + synthetic data is the differentiation.

## 9. Cyber-insurance carriers (rank #9)

**Why it (partially) aligns:** Insurers bundle *complimentary* ID-theft services as policyholder perks to reduce claims. A removal product reduces the attack surface (fewer broker listings → fewer targeted scams) — the insurer's economic interest. But insurers buy insurance policies and wholesale service capacity, not consumer SaaS — the deal shape is "vendor as claims-cost reducer," a long BD cycle for a solo founder.

**Evidence (with URLs):**
- **Chubb × CyberScout:** complimentary identity-management services via CyberScout to Masterpiece homeowners policyholders. https://canadianunderwriter.ca/2017/03/28/chubb-to-provide-complimentary-identity-management-services-to-canadian-personal-lines-customers/
- **Chubb UK personal cyber:** sold *only through partner businesses*; Chubb notes "a long history of providing white label and branded solutions for some of the world's leading brands" — the white-label precedent in insurance, but for the *policy itself*, offered to large partners. https://www.insurtechinsights.com/chubb-launches-uk-personal-cyber-product/
- **CyberScout master-policy precedent:** Malwarebytes bundles up to $1–2M identity-theft insurance "issued and covered by Lloyd's, under a master group policy issued in the name of Cyberscout Limited" — the playbook for a software company attaching an insurance component via a wholesale carrier relationship. https://www.malwarebytes.com/identity-theft-protection

## White-label vs. affiliate — trade-offs for Vanyshr

**Affiliate / referral (run Vanyshr's own program from day one):**
- Lowest trust bar, fastest to launch, partner keeps their brand, Vanyshr keeps its own.
- Category rate card is public: **$65–$125 CPA, 30–60-day cookies, Impact as the standard platform** (Aura's terms are the number to beat or match).
- Con: no control over the partner's UX, coupon-driven dynamics, affiliates optimize for volume not fit.

**Co-branded distribution ("powered by Vanyshr"):**
- The realistic partnership model for a solo founder: advisor perks, association member benefits, creator-org discounts.
- Industry norm in every proven example (Carefull deals, FOREWARN, Baird/InfoArmor) — nobody in these channels expects full white-label.
- Vanyshr keeps its brand intact while the partner owns the relationship.

**Full white-label / API embedding:**
- Proven model in this exact category: **Optery's white-label API** (partners embed scanning + removal "without building it from scratch"); PureVPN's white-label data-broker-removal product; Onerep powering privacy features "behind the scenes" for global brands.
- Demands what a solo founder doesn't have yet: SOC 2, Deloitte-grade audits, SLA capacity, enterprise trust posture.
- **Credibility landmine:** Privacy Guides' published criteria for data-broker-removal recommendations explicitly disqualify services that "must not be a white labeled service or reseller of another provider." Structure white-label deals so the *partner* owns the relationship while Vanyshr's own direct brand stays intact and independently recommendable.
- Sequencing: **affiliate first** (validates demand + unit economics) → **co-branded distribution** (associations, advisors, orgs) → **API white-label** once the trust posture exists.
